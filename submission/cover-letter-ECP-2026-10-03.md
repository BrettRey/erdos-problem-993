Dear Editor,

I submit "A variance-scaled Turán inequality at the first descent of a Poisson–binomial mass function" for consideration in *Electronic Communications in Probability*.

Let $W$ be a sum of independent Bernoulli variables with pmf $f$ and variance $V\geq1$, and let $D$ be the first index at which $f$ decreases. The paper proves $V(1-f_{D-1}f_{D+1}/f_D^2)\geq1/4$. Consequently $f_{D+r}/f_D\leq\exp(-r/(4V))$ past the first descent. A binomial family shows that no constant above $1/3$ is possible. The known lower bounds on this log-concavity deficit scale with the number of summands or with the success probabilities. Both degenerate at fixed variance when summands with success probabilities near 0 and near 1 accumulate. The new bound depends on the variance alone. Through Pitman's correspondence it also applies to the coefficient sequences of real-rooted polynomials with nonnegative coefficients, whenever the associated variance is at least one.

The proof reduces the theorem to a one-variable inequality. Part of that inequality is verified by exact rational Bernstein expansions: 275 coefficients in thirteen cells, generated by one program and rechecked by an independently written program that uses only the Python standard library. The supplementary archive contains both programs, a third program that checks the symbolic computations for the remaining range, the coefficient data, and replay instructions.
