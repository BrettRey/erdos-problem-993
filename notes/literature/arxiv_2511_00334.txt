                                                     INDEPENDENCE POLYNOMIALS OF TREES

                                                   Abstract. We construct infinite families of trees whose independence poly-
                                                   nomials violate log-concavity at an arbitrary number of indices. This affirma-
                                                   tively answers a question of D. Galvin.




                                                                               1. Introduction
                                              The conjecture that independence polynomials of trees are unimodal [1] was later
                                          strengthened to the more tractable conjecture that they are log-concave [9]. Several
                                          authors have since disproven this [7, 6, 4, 10]. At the time of this writing, all known
                                          counterexamples in the literature exhibited only a single break in log-concavity.
                                          This observation led Galvin [4] to ask whether multiple breaks are possible.
                                              In this note, we answer that question affirmatively. We construct infinite fam-
                                          ilies of trees whose independence polynomials break log-concavity at an arbitrary
                                          number of indices (Theorem 1).

                                                                       2. Definitions and Results
                                             We use standard definitions, notations, and concepts from the literature con-

   This analysis reveals how the log-concavity of I(T Gm,t ) is broken multiple times.

Theorem 1. For each positive integer m and for every sufficiently large integer t,
the log-concavity of the independence polynomial of the tree T Gm,t is broken at m
indices.
Proof. Let 0 ≤ k ≤ 2(m − 1). According to Lemma 3, ck (m, t) ∈ Θ(2uk t ), where
uk = k + ⌊k/2⌋. Let us assume that k is even and write k = 2j. Then, uk = 3j,
uk+1 = 3j + 1, uk+2 = 3(j + 1), and
             ck (m, t)ck+2 (m, t) ∈ Θ(2(6j+3)t ),     c2k+1 (m, t) ∈ Θ(2(6j+2)t ),
which implies
                     ck (m, t)ck+2 (m, t) − c2k+1 (m, t) ∈ Θ(2(6j+3)t ).
                                                                                             □
   For example, straightforward calculations using (4) and (3) show that the graphs
T G4,6 and T G5,6 break log-concavity exactly at indices 84, 82, 80, 78 and 105, 103,
101, 99, 97, respectively.
