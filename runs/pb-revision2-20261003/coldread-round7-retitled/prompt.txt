Variance and local log-concavity of Poisson–binomial laws*
Brett Reynolds†

Abstract
Let W be a finite sum of independent Bernoulli variables with probability mass
function f and variance V ≥ 1, and let D be the first index at which f decreases. The
normalized Turán deficit δk = 1 − fk−1 fk+1 /fk2 is a bounded transform of the discrete
curvature of log f at k , and for a normal density of variance V that curvature is 1/V .
We prove V δD ≥ 1/4, and an explicit family of binomial laws shows that no constant
above 1/3 is possible, so the best constant lies between 1/4 and 1/3. Cubic inequalities
of Hillion and Johnson then give δk ≥ 1/(4V + |k − D|) at every k in the support, and
at the rightmost mode c we have 1/(4V + 1) ≤ δc < 2/V . Ultra-log-concavity alone
gives no such bound. For the number of eigenvalues of an N × N Haar unitary matrix
in a half circle, the bound at D is of order 1/ log N , against order 1/N from ultra-logconcavity. The proof combines the cubic inequalities with a maximal-mass bound of
Bobkov, Marsiglietti, and Melbourne to reduce the main inequality to a one-variable
inequality, which is proved in exact arithmetic by Bernstein expansions.
Keywords: Poisson–binomial law; Bernoulli sum; log-concavity; Turán inequality; ultra-logconcavity; modal index; random unitary matrix; computer-assisted proof.
